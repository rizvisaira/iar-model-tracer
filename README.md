## Task and result

Each image was generated by one of eight class-conditional ImageNet models: RAR-B / L / XL / XXL [5] or VAR-d16 / d20 / d24 / d30 [6], or by an unknown model. The task is to assign one of 9 labels (`rarb rarl rarxl rarxxl var16 var20 var24 var30 outlier`) to each of 9,800 test images. Only 800 labelled training images (no outliers) and 450 validation images (including 50 outliers) are given, and the metric is accuracy.

**Final result on val (450 images):**

| metric | value |
|---|---|
| accuracy | **94.0%** (95% CI 91.8–96.0) |
| size accuracy given the correct family | **97.7%** (95% CI 96.1–99.0) |
| family accuracy (RAR / VAR / outlier) | 96.0% |
| outlier recall / precision | 88.0% / 86.3% |

The intervals are bootstrap intervals over the val images. Per stage, from [results/stages_val.csv](results/stages_val.csv):

| stage | method | accuracy | family acc. | outlier recall | outlier precision | size acc. given family |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | autoencoder signals, original encoders | 23.6% | 83.8% | 32.0% | 53.3% | 24.9% |
| 2 | autoencoder signals, fine-tuned inverse decoders | 31.6% | 96.0% | 88.0% | 86.3% | 25.3% |
| 3 | + token-habit size step (no family passed its criteria, so unchanged) | 31.6% | 96.0% | 88.0% | 86.3% | 25.3% |
| 4 | **+ generator-likelihood size step (final)** | **94.0%** | 96.0% | 88.0% | 86.3% | **97.7%** |

Chance for the size within a family is 25%. Of the 27 remaining val errors, 18 are family or outlier decisions (Stage 2) and 9 are sizes (Stage 4). See [results/final_summary.md](results/final_summary.md) and [notebooks/005_final_results.ipynb](notebooks/005_final_results.ipynb).

## Method

```text
image ─► inverse decoders D⁻¹ (RAR, VAR) ─► provenance losses ─► nearest family + threshold ─► RAR / VAR / outlier
                                                                                │ RAR or VAR
                                                                                ▼
          recovered tokens + top-5 ImageNet classes ─► log-likelihood under each of the family's 4 transformers
                                                                                ▼
                                                     logistic regression ─► size (e.g. rarxl, var24)
```

**Family and outliers come from the autoencoder.** Following Zhao et al. [1], each family's tokenizer (autoencoder) is used as a provenance check. An image made by a family's generator was decoded by that family's decoder, so encoding it again with an inverse of that decoder gives small quantisation and reconstruction losses. Images from the other family, or from unknown models, give larger losses. Each image goes to the family with the more typical loss, or is flagged as an outlier if it is not typical of either.

**The size comes from the generators.** All four sizes of a family share the same autoencoder, so autoencoder signals cannot tell them apart: Stages 1–2 stay at chance for the size. The sizes differ only in their transformer. The size step therefore recovers the image's tokens and asks each of the family's four transformers how likely those tokens are, under the same guided sampling distribution that its sampler uses. This builds on the per-token statistics used for membership inference on IARs by Kowalczuk et al. [2]: the difference between a model's outputs with and without the class, and Min-K%-style statistics over the lowest-probability tokens. Here they are used to attribute an image to a model rather than to detect training members. The model that generated the image should find its tokens most likely.

**Rules followed throughout:**
- Classifiers are trained on train or on our own generated images only, and val is used only for evaluation.
- Each stage's pass criteria were fixed before looking at its results.
- Nothing is tuned on test.

**Stage 1: original encoders** ([001](notebooks/001_stage_1.ipynb)). The tokenizers' own encoders serve as the inverse decoder. A nearest-family rule standardises each family's loss on its train images, and calls an image an outlier above the 95% quantile of the family's own train images. Family accuracy is 83.8%, but outlier recall only 32%. The size, from a logistic regression on the same signals, is at chance (24.9%). Accuracy: 23.6%.

**Stage 2: fine-tuned inverse decoders** ([002](notebooks/002_stage_2.ipynb)). As in [1], each family's inverse decoder is fine-tuned on images generated by its own models. This used a shortened recipe: 2,560 images per model and 10 / 5 epochs, about 2.5 h on an A40. The same rule then reaches 96.0% family accuracy, with 88% outlier recall at 86% precision. The size is still at chance (25.3%), which confirms that the autoencoder cannot see it. Accuracy: 31.6%.

**Stage 3: token habits, a negative result** ([003](notebooks/003_stage_3.ipynb)). Do the sizes write different tokens? Per-image statistics of the tokens were tested: histograms, entropy, distinct tokens, same-token neighbours, codebook rarity. Classifiers on these reach only 26–30% for the size, even on the generators' own true tokens. All sizes of a family write tokens with the same overall statistics, so the pre-registered criteria failed and the size step was not replaced.
- *Why it matters:* it rules out the cheap route, and it locates the signal. If the sizes differ, they must differ in *how probable each token is given its context and class*, not in which tokens appear. That is what Stage 4 tests.
- *What the criteria prevented:* they kept a chance-level classifier out of the final model.

**Stage 4: generator likelihood** ([004](notebooks/004_stage_4.ipynb)).
- *Inputs.* Tokens are recovered with Stage 2's fine-tuned inverse decoder. A pretrained ConvNeXt-L gives the 5 most likely ImageNet classes, since the generators are class-conditional and the class is unknown. Each of the family's four transformers then scores the tokens, under its own guided sampling distribution, rebuilt exactly from its repo's sampler. The settings are RAR's per-size guidance schedule and temperature, and VAR's classifier-free-guidance schedule with top-k / top-p.
- *Classifier.* The per-model scores are combined over the 5 classes. A logistic regression on the four models' likelihood summaries picks the size. It is trained on generated images only.
- *Results by setting:*
  - with the true tokens and class, it identifies the size of 100% of held-out generated images;
  - with recovered tokens and estimated classes, 99.5% (RAR) and 96.5% (VAR);
  - on the task's labelled images, 98.7% and 95.5%.
- *End to end.* Both families passed all pre-registered criteria, so the size step was replaced. Val accuracy rises from 31.6% to 94.0%, with family and outlier decisions unchanged.
- *What carries VAR.* The feature ablation in [005](notebooks/005_final_results.ipynb), Section 6, shows that for VAR the class-conditional log-likelihood carries most of the signal: 95.0% alone.

## Limitations

1. **The test set's outlier share appears to differ from val's.** The model calls 39.9% of test images outliers, against 11.3% of val (true share 11.1%).
   - *Evidence for a real difference.* The comparison in [005](notebooks/005_final_results.ipynb), Section 4, suggests a real difference in composition rather than a shift of the whole test set. The test z-scores are bimodal: one mode matches val's in-family images, the other val's outliers. The in-family part has val's quantiles. The lower tail implies an outlier share of 43–49%.
   - *Expected accuracy.* If val's per-group rates carry over, expected test accuracy is about 91.8% (95% CI 86.7–95.6).
   - *The other case.* If test instead had val's 11% outliers, about a third of its in-family images would have to be called outliers, and accuracy would be about 66.5%.
   - *Open.* Without test labels this cannot be settled, and test's outliers may come from generators unlike val's.
2. **The task's sampling settings are inferred, not given.** The guided distributions use each repo's ImageNet FID settings (RAR's per-size guidance and temperature; VAR's cfg 1.5, top-k 900, top-p 0.96). This is supported by the transfer check in [004](notebooks/004_stage_4.ipynb), Section 4: the likelihood tables of task images match our generated ones (correlation 1.000 / 0.999), and accuracy drops by at most 2.5 points from generated to task images. But it is an assumption.
3. **VAR's size depends on a learned combination of likelihood summaries.** Only 27% of VAR tokens are recovered correctly, against 85% for RAR. After recovery, the single highest likelihood picks the right VAR depth for only 66% of images.
   - *How the LR recovers it.* The logistic regression over the four models' summaries reaches 96.5% (generated) / 95.5% (task).
   - *Ablation.* The class-conditional log-likelihood alone gives 95.0% / 92.7%, and no single feature group is necessary. The gain comes mainly from weighing the four models against each other, learned on generated data.
   - *Residual on test.* Test images predicted VAR fit their predicted depth somewhat worse than val's: 4.2% with |z| > 3, against 1.0% on val. This is partly, but not fully, explained by outliers wrongly given a family.
4. **The family / outlier step is now the bottleneck.** It causes 18 of the 27 val errors. It uses only the Stage 2 autoencoder signals, and the inverse decoders used a shortened fine-tuning recipe (about 2.5 h on an A40, against about 3 days for the paper's full recipe). The generator likelihood is not used for outlier detection, though val's outliers fit the RAR models very badly, so it might help.
5. **No test labels, and a small val set.** All accuracies are measured on 450 val images, of which only 50 are outliers, so outlier rates have wide intervals.
6. **Cost.** The size step scores every image under all four models of its family with 5 candidate classes: about 1.1 s per image for RAR and 2.7 s for VAR on an A40.

for f in rar var; do
  /workspace/venv/bin/python scripts/score_likelihood.py --family $f --source task --tokens recovered --class-mode topk \
